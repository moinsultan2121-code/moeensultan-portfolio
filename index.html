My Take

Every metric has a story.
Find it before you scale it.

Don't just look at the number that moved. Ask why it moved: creative, audience, offer, or something upstream nobody checked yet.

Forget the campaign for a second and look at the whole path a customer actually took: click, landing page, offer, checkout, follow-up. Somewhere in there is the real bottleneck.

That's usually where the next dollar should go.

Perspective

Notes on paid media, funnels, and what actually moves numbers.

Why most "creative fatigue" is actually audience fatigue

The ad looks tired, but more often it's the audience that's worn out, not the creative. Refresh the segment before you refresh the hook.

The lead you didn't qualify is a lead you paid for twice

Every unqualified lead gets paid for once at the click and again when someone wastes an hour on it. Qualification is a budget decision, not just a CRM one.

ROAS is a vanity metric until you attach a margin to it

A 5x return on a product with thin margin can lose money quietly. Know the margin before you celebrate the multiple.

What tracking debt actually costs you

Broken pixels and mismatched conversions don't just skew the report, they misdirect the algorithm for weeks before anyone notices. Fix tracking first, optimize second.

The best performing ad is the one you didn't expect

The ad you were sure would flop is usually the one worth scaling. Let the data overrule your taste, every time.

Full-funnel thinking, one dashboard at a time

A single view from click to close changes what you optimize for. Most accounts are still guessing because nobody built that dashboard yet.
